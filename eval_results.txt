Q7: What is the core idiosyncrasy/problem the paper identifies with time-discretization in RL?
Answer: The core idiosyncrasy identified in the paper regarding time-discretization in reinforcement learning (RL) is the challenge that arises when naively applying a discrete-time algorithm to a discretized continuous-time environment. This issue stems from the fact that physical systems operate continuously, yet many RL algorithms assume fixed-duration, discrete time steps. The paper highlights that this discrepancy can lead to misalignments in the definitions of returns and emphasizes the need for modifications to better align these definitions, particularly in environments where the granularity of time discretization is a choice or is inherently stochastic (Source: rp3.pdf, Page: 0).

Q8: Why do physical systems create a challenge for fixed time-step RL algorithms?
Answer: Physical systems create a challenge for fixed time-step reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms because they are continuous in time, which necessitates a choice of time-discretization granularity when digitally controlling them. Additionally, these systems do not wait for decisions to be made before advancing the environment state, meaning that feedback for actions is delayed. This delay is compounded by hardware limitations on sampling rates, which further affects when a system can receive feedback for an action. Consequently, many existing robotics environments, where this issue is particularly relevant, compute rewards based on the next time step’s state information, complicating the learning process (Source: rp3.pdf, Page: 0-1).

Q12: What is the average-reward criterion, as opposed to other reward criteria in RL?
Answer: The average-reward criterion in reinforcement learning (RL) focuses on maximizing the long-term average of the expected rewards over time, known as the reward rate. This contrasts with discounted or total-reward scenarios, where the emphasis may be on maximizing the total reward over a finite horizon or applying a discount factor to future rewards. The average-reward criterion is particularly suitable for systems that require sustained performance and reliability over extended periods without operational resets (rp4.pdf, Page: 0).
